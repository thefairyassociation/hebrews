# Hebrews authorship: first reproducible investigation

16 October 2026

**We have not identified the author.** The concrete progress is a Greek-text
experiment that exposes the limits of a simple Paul-versus-Luke comparison,
checks whether chapter 13 drives that comparison, and separates ancient claims
about intellectual authorship from claims about the surviving wording.

## What we actually did

We analysed **117,399 Greek tokens across 16 books**, using a pinned
MorphGNT/SBLGNT corpus. Hebrews contains 4,935 tokens in this edition. Seven
conventionally undisputed Pauline letters form one reference group; Luke and
Acts form the other. Hebrews and six additional comparison texts never enter
training. The historical identification of Luke–Acts' literary author is a
separate question.

The experiment uses common grammatical words rather than theological keywords:
articles, conjunctions, prepositions and particles, with a separate setting
including pronouns. It varies normalized word forms versus lemmas, 30 versus
50 selected features, two distance measures, and full text versus a coarse
Old Testament quotation mask. That makes **32 analyses**, all reported.
Selections and scales use training books only. Each reference book contributes
equally to its group's average; even the 334-word Philemon is retained.

These settings were fixed before inspecting the numerical outcomes in this
session. This is exploratory work, not an independently registered study.
The [design](design.md), code and output tables expose the assumptions.

## Result 1: the winning name depends on how similarity is measured

| Text condition | Settings nearer Luke–Acts | Settings nearer Paul |
|---|---:|---:|
| Complete Hebrews, all verses | 11 | 5 |
| Complete Hebrews, Swete-listed verses removed | 10 | 6 |
| Hebrews 1–12, all verses | 12 | 4 |
| Hebrews 1–12, Swete-listed verses removed | 10 | 6 |

Thus complete Hebrews is nearer Luke–Acts in 21/32 comparisons and Paul in
11/32. **21/32 is not a probability that Luke wrote Hebrews.** The settings
reuse the same documents and closely related features; they are not independent
votes. No principled reason has been established for counting them equally as
historical evidence.

For a concrete reversal, using 50 lemma features without pronouns on full
Hebrews gives Hellinger distances of **0.10646 to Paul** and **0.11307 to
Luke–Acts**, favouring Paul. Standardizing those same features and measuring
mean absolute difference gives **0.85544 to Paul** and **0.81594 to Luke–Acts**,
favouring Luke–Acts. Each metric has its own scale; compare within a row, not
across metrics.

This is a sensitivity finding. It neither proves that all stylometry is useless
nor selects one of these two authors.

## Result 2: good reference-book accuracy is not an author identification

For each reference book, we removed the entire book before selecting features,
calculating group averages or fitting scales. The methods recovered the
conventional reference group for all nine held-out books in **27/32 settings**,
and eight of nine in the other five. Every miss concerned 1 Thessalonians.

That apparently strong performance is insufficient. It tests discrimination
between two supplied literary groups, not whether a new text belongs to either.
There are only two Luke–Acts books; holding one out leaves one for that family,
while holding out a Pauline letter leaves six. These are limited, asymmetric
checks, not a general accuracy estimate for ancient authorship.

The non-training comparisons show what forced choice does:

| Comparison text | Nearer Luke–Acts | Nearer Paul |
|---|---:|---:|
| James | 24 | 8 |
| 1 Peter | 0 | 32 |
| 1 John | 22 | 10 |
| Matthew | 32 | 0 |
| Mark | 32 | 0 |
| John | 32 | 0 |

These are **similarity labels, not proposed reattributions of these books**.
The model has no independently calibrated “neither” category. In particular,
1 Peter's stable Pauline proximity illustrates why a Paul-like score for
Hebrews cannot establish common authorship. Genre, source use and shared
Christian idiom are plausible contributors; this experiment does not isolate
which one causes each comparison result.

## Result 3: the personal ending is not the sole source of the similarity

Removing all of Hebrews 13 changes the nearest reference family in only **3/32
paired comparisons**, leaving 29 unchanged. Two switch from Paul to Luke–Acts;
one switches the other way. The body therefore retains the mixed similarity
pattern without the chapter containing Timothy and the greetings from Italy.

This weakens one narrow explanation of these numerical results: that the
apparent relationships arise solely from Hebrews' epistolary ending. It does
**not** show that chapter 13 and the body share an author, that the chapter was
added, or that its personal details are historical. Removing an entire chapter
is a coarse ablation, not a test of the exact epilogue boundaries.

The source-derived quotation mask removes **1,024 tokens in 58 Hebrews verses**,
leaving 3,911. Those 1,024 are words in masked verses, not a precise count of
quoted words. The same mask rule applies to all reference and comparison books.
Only one complete-Hebrews setting changes its nearer family between full and
masked text. But the mask omits some repeated quotations and removes authorial
framing elsewhere. Stability under it does not eliminate scriptural influence.
See the [full annotation audit](quotation-mask.md).

Fixed 500- and 1,000-token chunks provide additional descriptive results. Their
frequencies, boundaries and discarded tails are published. They are not treated
as independent replications or used to inflate the reference sample size.

## What the historical evidence changes

The early evidence supports distinguishing **the source of ideas**, **the
composer of the Greek**, and **later association with a Pauline collection**.
Those need not identify the same person.

- **Origen:** his discussion preserved in Eusebius distinguishes Pauline
  thought from an uncertain literary hand. His *Letter to Africanus* §9 also
  explicitly defends the letter's Pauline attribution. His uncertainty cannot
  safely be reduced to “Origen proved Paul had nothing to do with it.”
- **Barnabas:** Tertullian's *On Modesty* 20 explicitly associates Hebrews with
  Barnabas and quotes Hebrews 6. This is real early attribution testimony,
  although its earlier transmission chain is unknown. It is not evidence that
  the separate *Epistle of Barnabas* supplies authenticated training prose.
- **Clement of Rome:** resemblance between 1 Clement and Hebrews needs a
  borrowing control. Eusebius himself discusses literary borrowing as well as
  resemblance. Shared wording is not automatically an individual fingerprint.

The [historical dossier](historical-evidence.md) gives the precise primary
references and dependencies; the [internal-evidence note](internal-evidence.md)
checks Hebrews 2:3, 11:32 and 13:22–25 without changing interpretive rules to
favour a particular candidate.

Apollos fits a broad biographical profile in Acts 18, but we have no authenticated
writing sample from him here. The same problem applies to Priscilla and the
apostolic Barnabas. A statistical rejection of Paul or Luke would not identify
one of those candidates. An unrecorded author remains possible.

## What is new here, and what is not

Greek authorship analysis of Hebrews is established prior work. The 2013
[Ebrahimpour et al. paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0054998) itself
obtained different outcomes from different classifiers. We do not reproduce
those classifiers or claim to refute their work. Erickson's
[2024 thesis](https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/honors/1392/) also reports
limited evidence for Luke–Acts proximity. Pracht and McCauley's
[2025 study](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel16010055) investigates the transmission
of style through early Christian literary networks.

The new contribution **to this repository** is an auditable set of analyses
with whole-book holdouts, explicit out-of-reference comparisons, matched
quotation rules and chapter-13 sensitivity. It narrows what this class of
simple comparison can responsibly claim. No priority or discovery claim is made.

## Verification

An independent audit reconstructs all **544 whole-text distances** from the raw
Greek records and masks; checks feature selection, means, standard deviations
and centroids for **160 training models**; and verifies **5,408 complete chunk
profiles**, contribution sums and tails. Perturbing target/control or held-out
profiles leaves the corresponding training models unchanged. A corrupted corpus
file is rejected. Two complete analysis runs produced byte-identical output
files, including the compressed frequency table. This verifies the computation,
not the historical assumptions or completeness of the quotation annotation.

All checks are reproducible using the commands in the [README](../README.md).

## Best next discriminating work

1. **Broaden the comparison corpus and permit rejection.** Add 1 Clement with
   overlaps removed, other early Christian exhortation, and suitable
   non-Christian Greek controls. Calibrate an unseen-author rejection procedure
   on independently attributed writers and matched genres before applying it
   to Hebrews. This is more useful than merely increasing classifier complexity.
2. **Improve quotation and genre annotation.** Replace the coarse verse mask
   with independently checked word-level boundaries. Compare discourse roles
   and relevant speech/exhortation passages without assuming the model has
   learned authorship rather than genre.
3. **Check the Latin Barnabas tradition.** The historical dossier identifies
   a later Latin quotation attributed to Barnabas as a lead. Verify it in the
   critical edition and investigate dependence on Tertullian before treating it
   as another witness. Independent corroboration would strengthen an ancient
   attribution tradition, not by itself prove authorship.

For now, Luke–Acts is a worthwhile comparison corpus, Barnabas deserves careful
historical investigation, and no candidate has earned an identification from
this experiment.
